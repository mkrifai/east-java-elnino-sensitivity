# Public Summary: El Niño Drought in East Java

**Key findings from 25 years of satellite research (2001–2025)**

---

## What is the problem?

Every few years, a natural climate phenomenon called **El Niño** causes prolonged drought in East Java. Rainfall drops sharply, soil dries out, wells run low, and rice crops fail completely (*puso*). The impact is devastating for smallholder farmers who depend on their paddy fields as their sole source of income.

Until now, help has only arrived **after** the damage is done. Government only sends water pumps and emergency funds when crops have already yellowed and died. The question is: *Can we know earlier, and act before it's too late?*

---

## What did this research find?

This study analysed **25 years of satellite data** to map the impact of 8 El Niño events across East Java. Five key findings:

### 1. El Niño always hits the same places
Across 8 El Niño events in 25 years, **the severely affected areas are always the same**: the northern corridor (Lamongan, Bojonegoro, Tuban, Gresik) and the lower Brantas River valley. About **35% of East Java** suffers severe drought during every episode.

### 2. Six regencies bear almost all the damage
During the 2023–2024 El Niño, **41,576 hectares** of farmland were destroyed in East Java. Of that, **92% was concentrated in just 6 regencies**:

| Regency | Damage | Share of province |
|:---|---:|---:|
| Lamongan | 12,997 ha | 31% |
| Gresik | 6,008 ha | 14% |
| Pacitan | 5,880 ha | 14% |
| Bojonegoro | 5,747 ha | 14% |
| Tulungagung | 4,426 ha | 11% |
| Tuban | 3,393 ha | 8% |

### 3. Small regencies can suffer more than large ones
Although Lamongan lost the most hectares, **Pacitan lost 43% of all its paddy land** — nearly half! Lamongan still had 85% of its paddies surviving. This means severity cannot be measured by hectares alone — we must also look at what percentage of a community's farmland was destroyed.

### 4. Early warning is available 1 month before crops die
Satellites can detect **leaf water content dropping** about 4–6 weeks before leaves visibly turn yellow. This time window is enough to send water pumps, adjust irrigation schedules, and save harvests.

### 5. The satellite model matches real-world disaster data
The vulnerability map created from satellites was compared against official BPBD data on villages experiencing water crises in 2023. Result: **87% of the variation in real crisis events can be explained by this satellite map**.

## What does this mean for me and my community?

**If you are a farmer in an affected regency:**
- Register with your **Farmer Group** (*Kelompok Tani*) and make sure you are enrolled in **Paddy Crop Insurance (AUTP)** — so you receive compensation if your harvest fails.
- When BMKG announces El Niño (usually April–May), **move your planting schedule forward** and choose drought-resistant rice varieties (e.g. Inpari 30, Inpago).
- **Do not burn land** to clear fields during the dry season — the risk of uncontrollable fire is very high.

**If you are a city resident or consumer:**
- El Niño can cause **rice prices to rise** due to reduced supply. Be aware and budget accordingly.
- Conserve household water during the dry season.

**If you are a village leader:**
- Check whether your village falls within the **high vulnerability zone** (available in this study's public repository).
- Coordinate with the Regency Agriculture Service to obtain seed assistance and water pumps *before* the dry season peaks.

---

## What should the government do?

This research recommends 4 concrete steps:

1. **Direct 70% of drought emergency funds to the 10 most vulnerable regencies** — based on the risk map, not equal distribution.
2. **Use satellites for early warning** — monitor crop moisture from satellites every 8 days, and send water pumps as soon as danger signals are detected.
3. **Make crop insurance (AUTP) free** for all farmers in the 6 worst-affected regencies when El Niño is announced.
4. **Regulate river water sharing** fairly between upstream and downstream areas during compound events (El Niño + positive IOD).
